### 5. Manny Medina (guest post). "A new framework for AI agent pricing." Growth Unhinged, Apr 9, 2025. [V] (public portion)
URL: https://www.growthunhinged.com/p/ai-agent-pricing-framework
- Four AI agent pricing models: (1) per agent (FTE replacement; fixed monthly fee, headcount budget); (2) per agent action (consumption; tokens plus margin, per minute); (3) per agent workflow (per completed multi-step workflow with a clear deliverable; risk of negative margin on long runs); (4) per agent outcome (results-based).
- Best-for: agent = broad jobs, predictable workload, clear labor savings; action = varied discrete tasks, unpredictable volume; workflow = multi-step processes with intermediate deliverables; outcome = predictable performance plus clearly defined success metrics, in markets that already expect it.
- Outcome-pricing prerequisites: confidence the agent consistently delivers; clear success metric; attribution method (A/B or proof of concept); larger, longer contracts (over one year) to absorb bespoke contract overhead.
- Outcome pricing: highest alignment, lowest price-compression risk, but custom contracts proliferate. Intercom uses standardized outcomes, Sierra bespoke ones (per author). [S on companies]

### 11. Sawtooth Software. Van Westendorp Pricing Sensitivity Meter (blog). sawtoothsoftware.com, accessed 2026-10-30. [V for method description; Sawtooth is a research-software vendor]
URL: https://sawtoothsoftware.com/resources/blog/posts/van-westendorp-pricing-sensitivity-meter
- Developed in the 1970s by Dutch economist Peter van Westendorp (commonly dated to a 1976 ESOMAR congress paper; year not on this page, flag).
- Four questions (paraphrase for the book): at what price would this be so cheap you would doubt its quality; a bargain; getting expensive but still worth considering; so expensive you would not consider it.
- Cumulative curves intersect at: point of marginal cheapness (PMC, leftmost), optimal price point (OPP, "too cheap" crosses "too expensive"), indifference price point (IPP, "cheap" crosses "expensive"), point of marginal expensiveness (PME, rightmost). Acceptable range PMC to PME.
- Limits: no competitive context, weak theoretical basis for line crossings, does not predict purchase volume, ignores cost and positioning.
- Newton, Miller and Smith extension (1993): two purchase-intent questions at the "cheap" and "expensive" prices, calibrated (Sawtooth's example weights 75%, 25%, 10% by intent level) to estimate demand and revenue.
- B2B use: early concept range finding with buyers (Marcus) who know budgets; never as the sole input. Works poorly for brand-new categories where buyers lack reference prices.

### 12. Sawtooth Software. Gabor-Granger pricing method (blog). sawtoothsoftware.com, accessed 2026-10-30. [V]
URL: https://sawtoothsoftware.com/resources/blog/posts/gabor-granger-pricing-method
- Economists Andre Gabor and Clive Granger (1960s; year not on page, flag).
- Respondent gets a randomized start price; asked purchase intent; price moves up after yes, down after no. Output: demand curve (share willing at each price), revenue curve (share times price), revenue-maximizing price; profit-maximizing if costs known.
- Limits: one product in isolation, no attribute trade-offs, assumes rational responses and stable market.
- Use: repricing or launch where a direct price-sensitivity read is enough; conjoint when trade-offs or competitors matter.

### 13. Sawtooth Software. Conjoint analysis overview. sawtoothsoftware.com, accessed 2026-10-30. [V]
URL: https://sawtoothsoftware.com/conjoint-analysis
- Quantifies the value buyers place on attributes by having them choose among realistic product concepts (choice-based conjoint, CBC, most used; adaptive CBC for long attribute lists, about two to three times longer). Usually 8 to 20 choice tasks.
- Outputs: part-worth utilities, attribute importances (sensitive to level ranges), willingness to pay and price sensitivity, market simulator for what-if configurations versus competitors.
- Key advantage: infers WTP from trade-offs instead of asking directly.
- B2B caution [editorial]: small buyer populations, buying committees and negotiated prices limit sample size and realism; often run with users or influencers, then triangulated with deal data.

### 14. Sawtooth Software. MaxDiff overview. sawtoothsoftware.com, accessed 2026-10-30. [V]
URL: https://sawtoothsoftware.com/maxdiff
- MaxDiff (best-worst scaling): respondents see 4 or 5 items per set and pick most and least important; usually 8 to 15 sets; balanced design.
- Outputs: scores on a common 0 to 100 scale (sum to 100), individual-level scores (hierarchical Bayes), segments (latent class), TURF for best portfolio of items.
- Better discrimination than rating scales; avoids scale-use bias.
- Pricing use [editorial]: rank features to sort leaders, fillers and killers before tiering; Sawtooth lists package design and product-line optimization as uses. Origin usually credited to Jordan Louviere (around 1987 to 1992); not on this page, flag.

### 16. Zendesk. "Zendesk introduces Outcome-Based Pricing for AI agents" (newsroom), Aug 28, 2024 [V]; and Zendesk Help Center, "About automated resolutions" articles, accessed 2026-10-30 [V].
URLs: https://www.zendesk.com/newsroom/articles/zendesk-outcome-based-pricing/ ; https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/6970583409690 ; https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/5352026794010
- Customers pay for "automated resolutions": issues resolved autonomously by AI without escalation to a human. Plans include an allowance; more can be bought.
- The best public model of an operational outcome definition (use as the template for Beacon):
  - Unit: per conversation, not per user.
  - Eligibility: an identified use case plus a provided response or sufficient knowledge answer.
  - Exclusion: any conversation escalated to a human is not evaluated.
  - Verification: an LLM reviews the conversation to confirm the issue was resolved without human involvement; failures not counted.
  - Evaluation windows: email 72 hours after first email; messaging 2 hours by default (extendable to 72); voice at hangup.
  - Further exclusions: admin testing, sandbox, certain recommendation APIs and channels.
  - Transparency: usage dashboard showing automated resolutions and automation rate; customers must configure detection of human replies or resolutions may be miscounted.
- Teaching point: an outcome price needs (1) a unit, (2) eligibility rules, (3) exclusions, (4) a verification method, (5) a time window, (6) a customer-visible audit trail, (7) a dispute path. Zendesk publishes 1 to 6; the chapter can note the dispute path is not described on these pages.

### 17. Wikipedia. "Price elasticity of demand." Accessed 2026-10-30. [S; standard economics, safe as concept]
URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Price_elasticity_of_demand
- Elasticity = percent change in quantity demanded divided by percent change in price; usually negative, quoted in absolute value. Example: elasticity of 2 means a 1% price rise cuts quantity about 2%.
- Elastic (above 1), inelastic (below 1), unit elastic (1). Inelastic: price rise raises revenue. Elastic: price rise lowers revenue. Revenue peaks where elasticity equals 1.
- Determinants: substitutes, necessity, time horizon (more elastic over time), share of budget, brand loyalty, who pays (someone else paying makes demand less elastic).
- B2B translation [editorial]: in B2B the measurable "quantity" is rarely units; it is win rate, deal cycle length, discount depth, seats or usage per account, expansion, downgrade and churn. "Measuring business impact of a price change" = watch these leading and lagging indicators against a baseline and holdout, not a demand curve.

## (a) Key concepts and frameworks (synthesis for the chapter)

1. **Design the product around the price (Ramanujam and Tacke, Monetizing Innovation, 2016).** Price is a design input, not a launch-week decision. Hold willingness-to-pay conversations during discovery, segment by needs and WTP, choose the monetization model and value metric, then scope features and tiers to hit the target price. Four failure types (feature shock, minivation, hidden gem, undead) give PMs a diagnostic vocabulary. Nine rules map to chapters 4 to 12 (WTP talk early; segment; configuration and bundling; beyond the price point: monetization models; pricing strategy (penetration, skimming, maximization); outside-in business case; communicate value; behavioral tactics; price integrity). B2B application: add three WTP questions to every concept interview with the economic buyer, and refuse a business case with no WTP evidence.

2. **WTP interview guide (synthesis; attribute the stance to Ramanujam and Tacke, the question forms to Van Westendorp and Gabor-Granger).** Template for the artifact:
   - Context: current cost of the problem (time, people, tools, incidents) in the buyer's own numbers.
   - Value: which capabilities matter most and least (forced ranking, a qualitative cousin of MaxDiff).
   - Metric: "How would you expect to pay for this? per user, per environment, per incident, per outcome?" and "What would make a bill feel unfair?"
   - Range (Van Westendorp style): too cheap to trust; a bargain; getting expensive; too expensive.
   - Point check (Gabor-Granger style): "At X per month, would you buy?" then move up or down.
   - Budget: which budget line pays (software, services, headcount), who approves, procurement thresholds.
   - Predictability: cap, commit or pay-as-you-go preference.
   - Do not: ask "what would you pay?" cold; pitch before asking; anchor with your own number first. Run with economic buyers (Marcus), not only users (Priya). AI harness role: synthesize transcripts into a WTP range and metric preferences per segment, with quotes as evidence.

3. **Value metric (Poyar 2021 criteria).** The unit the price scales with. Five tests: correlates with value customers see; shares in customer success and does not discourage adoption; lets customers start small and scale; grows over time for the average customer; can be monitored and metered. Add two B2B tests from AI-era experience (editorial, supported by Cursor and Poyar 2025 to 2026): predictable enough for procurement to budget, and hard to game or dispute. Value metric assessment artifact: score candidate metrics (seats, environments, alerts ingested, incidents handled, incidents resolved, actions executed) against the seven tests.

4. **Packaging: good-better-best, bundles, add-ons.** Good-better-best tiers built from segments (Ramanujam rule 2 and 3). Leaders, fillers, killers heuristic (Simon-Kucher practitioners [S]) to place features; MaxDiff to rank features quantitatively. Monetize team and org capabilities (SSO, audit, admin) in higher tiers (Poyar). AI add-ons became bundled into core tiers with price rises in 2025 (PricingSaaS data, source 21). In 2026, tier gates increasingly differ by consumption capacity, model access and speed (Metronome, source 25). Add-ons fit capabilities with narrow but intense demand (for Beacon: EU data residency pack, MSP multi-tenant console).

5. **Pricing research methods (conceptual).**
   - Van Westendorp PSM (1970s): four price-perception questions, acceptable range and indicative points; quick, no competitive context, not a demand forecast. Newton, Miller and Smith 1993 extension adds purchase intent.
   - Gabor-Granger (Gabor and Granger): sequential yes/no at prices; demand and revenue curves; single product in isolation.
   - Conjoint (choice-based): trade-offs among realistic packages including price; part-worths, WTP, simulator; heavier, needs sample size.
   - MaxDiff (best-worst scaling): feature importance ranking for packaging, not price.
   - B2B reality: small samples and buying committees; triangulate with win/loss, deal desk data and discount patterns; AI harness can draft surveys, simulate respondent logic, and summarize, but cannot replace real respondents.

6. **Price elasticity and measuring a price change (standard economics; break-even formula commonly taught in Nagle and Holden, The Strategy and Tactics of Pricing [U, not fetched]).**
   - Elasticity concept: percent change in quantity over percent change in price; inelastic means price rise raises revenue.
   - Break-even volume (in words): for a price cut, required volume increase equals the cut divided by (contribution margin minus the cut); for a price rise, tolerable volume loss equals the rise divided by (margin plus the rise). Worked example: at a 70% margin, a 10% cut needs about 16.7% more volume to keep contribution flat; a 10% rise breaks even even if volume falls 12.5%. (Arithmetic checked: 0.10 / 0.60 = 0.1667; 0.10 / 0.80 = 0.125.)
   - B2B "quantity" is win rate, deal size, discount depth, cycle length, expansion, downgrades, churn and usage per account.
   - Price change impact plan template: hypothesis; scope (new logos, renewals, segments); baseline period; holdout or phased rollout where legal and fair; leading indicators (pricing-page and quote conversion, win rate, discount depth, sales cycle, deals lost on price with Poyar's 20% heuristic); lagging indicators (ARR, NRR, GRR, gross margin, churn reasons); guardrails and kill criteria; grandfathering and notice policy; review dates. AI harness prompt pattern: "Compare win rate, average discount and cycle length for quotes issued 90 days before and after the change, by segment, and flag confounders such as a new competitor or a sales comp change."

7. **Discount governance (concept: pocket price waterfall, Marn and Rosiello, HBR 1992 [V for article, U for exact definitions]).** List price minus on-invoice discounts equals invoice price; minus off-invoice leakage (rebates, free services, extended payment terms, free months, credits) equals pocket price. Discount policy template: (1) price book and floor per tier; (2) approval matrix by discount depth (rep, manager, deal desk, finance or CRO), by deal size and by non-standard terms; (3) give-get list (multi-year, prepay, case study and publicity rights, auto-renewal, annual uplift; Poyar and Common Paper, source 27); (4) never discount the value metric unit rate without a volume commitment; (5) expiry of discounts at renewal and renewal uplift rules; (6) quarter-end controls; (7) monthly reporting of pocket price band by segment and rep. For AI usage: cap free credits and pilot credits with expiry dates.

8. **AI pricing models in 2026 (comparison artifact).**
   - Seat: predictable; misaligned when agents replace work (seat count shrinks). Investor preference 5% (Poyar 2026).
   - Usage (tokens, calls, actions): tracks cost; unpredictable for buyers; commoditizes if priced on tokens.
   - Credits: abstract many actions into one pool; flexible for vendor; black box for buyer (Poyar). Adoption 29% (Poyar 2026). Three credit types: compute proxy, value bundle, access gate (Metronome).
   - Outcome: price per resolved conversation or completed workflow; best alignment; needs reliable performance, clear metric, attribution and auditable verification (Medina framework, Zendesk, Intercom, HubSpot).
   - Hybrid: platform or seat fee plus included allowance plus usage, credits or outcome layer. Adoption 37% (Poyar 2026), investor preference 35%.
   - Medina's four agent models: per agent, per action, per workflow, per outcome.
   - Selection questions: what does the buyer value and can it be measured and attributed; how variable is our cost per unit; how predictable must the bill be; which budget pays; what is our confidence in performance; can the customer audit it.

9. **Metering and spend controls.** Included allowances, explicit budget opt-in for overage (GitHub), degraded fallback instead of hard stop (GitHub), alerts at thresholds, caps per team or project, auto-recharge, rollover vs expiry, real-time burn dashboard, overage policy "finish task then bill" vs "stop at zero" (Metronome), refunds and plain-language units (Cursor lesson). Metering must be accurate, explainable and auditable before any outcome price ships.

10. **Outcome definition template (synthesized from Zendesk and HubSpot published definitions).** Unit; eligibility; exclusions; verification method; evaluation window; customer-visible audit trail; dispute and credit-back process; what happens on harm. Price micro-outcomes, not macro-outcomes (Metronome on HubSpot).

## (e) Beacon worked example ideas (consistent with beacon-case.md)

Fixed facts: ARR about $48M, 38% growth, about 420 enterprise customers, NRR 118%, gross margin fell from 81% to 72% after agentic features because of inference cost; pricing is a platform fee per environment plus usage, moving to per-resolved-incident for Beacon Agents; North Star is incidents resolved with Beacon's help per week; personas Priya, Dana, Marcus, Sam, Leo; storyline of a hallucinated root cause causing a wrong rollback.

1. **Why move.** Per-environment pricing does not track value (one environment can produce 5 or 500 incidents) and does not track cost (inference scales with incidents diagnosed and actions run), which is why margin fell to 72%. Marcus wants an ROI story tied to MTTR and headcount leverage; the metric that matches the North Star is a resolved incident.
2. **Value metric assessment.** Score candidates (environments, alerts ingested, incidents correlated, diagnoses delivered, actions executed, incidents resolved) on the seven tests. Likely outcome: incidents resolved wins on value alignment and North Star fit, loses on predictability and dispute risk; actions executed wins on cost alignment; environments wins on predictability. Recommend hybrid.
3. **Proposed model (hybrid).** Platform fee per environment (keeps Correlate and Diagnose, predictable ARR, Sam's governance features in higher tiers) plus a per-resolved-incident price for Beacon Agents with an annual commit of a pool of resolved incidents and tiered rates; overage billed after the incident finishes, never stopping mid-incident; alerts at 75%, 90% and 100% of commit; caps per business unit. Leo (MSP) gets per-tenant metering and invoices.
4. **Outcome definition (Zendesk/HubSpot template).** A resolved incident is an incident Beacon correlated where (a) Beacon's diagnosis was accepted by a responder or its approved runbook action completed, (b) the incident closed within a defined window (for example 24 hours) without reopening within 72 hours, and (c) no human escalation outside Beacon's recommended path. Exclusions: test and sandbox environments, duplicate incidents merged later, incidents closed by a human with a different root cause, incidents caused by customer maintenance windows. Verification: evidence record per incident (diagnosis, acceptance, action log, closure), visible in a customer dashboard; LLM review plus responder confirmation. Disputes: customer flags within 30 days; credit-back if upheld.
5. **Credits when the agent causes incidents.** Tie to the hallucinated-root-cause storyline: if a Beacon action or accepted wrong diagnosis causes or extends an incident (for example the wrong rollback), that incident is not billable, the follow-on incident is not billable, and the customer receives a service credit (for example equal to N resolved incidents) per a published "agent-caused incident" policy. This turns trust into a pricing term; Sam and procurement will ask for it in contract language.
6. **WTP research plan.** 20 to 30 interviews across segments (enterprise direct, MSP via Leo, EU customers), Van Westendorp style range questions on price per resolved incident, Gabor-Granger point checks on commit tiers, MaxDiff on add-ons (EU AI Act documentation pack, multi-tenant console, autonomous allowlist expansion), and a small conjoint with Marcus-type buyers if sample allows. AI harness synthesizes transcripts into segment WTP ranges.
7. **Price change impact plan.** Grandfather existing per-environment contracts to renewal; offer migration with a 12-month price-protection guarantee; pilot with design partners; metrics: gross margin per resolved incident (target toward 80%), win rate, discount depth, NRR, deals lost on price, disputes per 1,000 billed incidents, customer-reported bill surprise. Kill or revise criteria if disputes exceed a threshold or win rate drops past a set band.
8. **Discount policy.** Floors on per-incident rate; deeper discounts only for larger commits or multi-year; free pilot credits expire in 60 days; MSP partner margin via wholesale rate, not ad hoc discounts; deal desk approval above a set depth.
9. **Pricing formula exercise.** Using fictional Beacon numbers chosen by the author: if contribution margin on Agents is 60% and Beacon cuts the per-incident rate 10%, volume of resolved incidents must rise about 20% (0.10 / 0.50) to hold contribution. Show gross margin per resolved incident as the unit economics line (inference plus tooling cost per incident).
10. **Avoiding the Cursor failure.** Communicate units in plain language ("one resolved incident"), never say "unlimited," default caps on, preview the first three months of bills in shadow mode before charging.
