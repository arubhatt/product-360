### 2. FTC. "FTC Order Requires Workado to Back Up Artificial Intelligence Detection Claims." Press release, April 28, 2025. [V]
URL: https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/04/ftc-order-requires-workado-back-artificial-intelligence-detection-claims
- Workado (formerly Content at Scale AI) claimed its AI Content Detector was "98 percent" accurate. [V]
- Independent testing: 53 percent accuracy on general-purpose content. Model was allegedly trained or fine-tuned only on academic content, though marketed as broad. [V]
- Order: no effectiveness claims unless not misleading and supported by "competent and reliable evidence" at the time the claim is made; must retain the evidence; notify customers; compliance reports for four years. Vote 3-0 (Ferguson-era Commission). [V]
- This is the cleanest model for a claims substantiation log: evidence must exist when the claim is made, must match the population the claim covers (general content vs. academic content), and must be kept. Strong analogue for B2B AI accuracy claims ("95% root-cause accuracy").

### 3. FTC. "FTC Reopens and Sets Aside Rytr Final Order in Response to the Trump Administration's AI Action Plan." Press release, December 22, 2025. [V]

### 9. April Dunford. "Shifting Positioning When AI Capabilities are Rapidly Changing." Substack, September 3, 2026. [V]
URL: https://aprildunford.substack.com/p/shifting-positioning-when-ai-capabilities

### 9b. April Dunford. "A Quick Guide to Positioning" (guest post). Lenny's Newsletter, January 26, 2021. [V]
URL: https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/positioning

- Order: start with competitive alternatives (what customers would do if your product did not exist); then what you have that alternatives do not; then ask "so what?" to get value; then the customers who care most about that value; finally the market category that makes that value obvious to them. - Ten-step process (book): understand the customers who love you; form a positioning team; align vocabulary and let go of positioning baggage; list true competitive alternatives; isolate unique attributes; map attributes to value themes; determine who cares a lot; find a market frame of reference that puts strengths at the center; layer on a trend carefully; capture positioning so it can be shared. [S, flag: verify against the book]
- Three styles of market positioning: head to head (win an existing category), big fish in a small pond (dominate a subsegment), create a new category. [S; "three styles" verified on the book page, names from the book]

### 10. Wikipedia. "Crossing the Chasm." Accessed 2026-10-30. [S]
URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crossing_the_Chasm
- Geoffrey A. Moore, first published 1991; revised 1999; third edition 2014. [S]
- Chasm = gap between early adopters (enthusiasts, visionaries) and the early majority (pragmatists). Advice: focus on one customer group at a time and use it as a base for the next; build the whole product for one problem in one vertical before moving to adjacent ones. [S]
- From the book (author's knowledge, not on this page): the D-Day analogy (pick a beachhead segment, concentrate force, then expand); the "bowling alley" of adjacent niches; target-customer characterization; the "elevator test"; the two-sentence positioning claim. [S, flag]
- Moore's positioning statement template (Crossing the Chasm; widely reproduced, not verified on a primary page this session) [S, flag]:
  For (target customers)
  Who (statement of the need or opportunity)
  The (product name) is a (product category)
  That (statement of key benefit, that is, compelling reason to buy)
  Unlike (primary competitive alternative)
  Our product (statement of primary differentiation)
- How a B2B PM applies it: the template is a compression of the Dunford analysis, not a substitute for it. Fill it last. The "Unlike" line must name the real alternative (often the status quo), and the "is a" line is the market category decision.

### Message house (general practitioner tool; no single originator found) [U, flag]
- Common structure: roof = umbrella message (one sentence, the positioning made external); pillars = three supporting messages, usually one per value theme; each pillar carries proof points (data, customer evidence, third-party validation); foundation = the facts about the company and product that make the claims credible (and, for AI, the trust and governance facts). Origin in PR and corporate communications; do not attribute to a person.
- Mapping: Dunford value themes become pillars; Dunford proof becomes proof points; Moore's "That" line becomes the roof. Every proof point in the house must have a row in the claims substantiation log.

### 17. FTC. "FTC Policy Statement Regarding Advertising Substantiation." November 23, 1984 (appended to Thompson Medical Co., 104 F.T.C. 648). [V]
URL: https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/ftc-policy-statement-regarding-advertising-substantiation
- Core rule: advertisers must have a reasonable basis for objective claims before they disseminate them; lacking one is unfair or deceptive under Section 5. [V]
- Level of substantiation depends on: type of claim; product; consequences of a false claim; benefits of a truthful claim; cost of developing substantiation; what experts in the field consider reasonable. (Commonly called the Pfizer factors; the page does not use that name.) [V]
- Express claims of a level of support ("tests prove", "studies show") require at least that level of support; if the ad implies more support than it states, the advertiser needs the support actually communicated. [V]
- Chapter use: this is the backbone of the claims substantiation log. "Cuts MTTR by 40%" is an express, objective, quantified claim and needs evidence in hand, on the relevant population, before it ships in a deck or on a web page.

## (a) Key concepts and frameworks (synthesis for the chapter)

1. **Positioning (April Dunford).** Positioning is the context-setting decision about how a product is different and better than alternatives for a particular set of customers. Five components worked in order: competitive alternatives, differentiated capabilities, value (with proof), best-fit customers (target segment), market category. Optional layer: a relevant trend, used carefully. It is an internal decision that messaging expresses. PM application: the PM brings the evidence (win/loss, discovery interviews, usage data, the Chapter 01 hypothesis results), the PMM runs the session and owns the words. Do positioning per offer scope decision (product vs. suite vs. company). Pre-launch positioning is a thesis.
2. **Three styles of market positioning (Dunford).** Head to head in an existing category; big fish in a small pond (own a subsegment of an existing category); create a new category. Most B2B products should start with the first two.
3. **Sales Pitch (Dunford, 2023).** Eight-step pitch built from positioning: insight about the market; the alternatives and their limitations; the perfect world; the product introduced; differentiated value; proof; objection handling; next step. Core idea: B2B buyers fear making the wrong decision more than they want to buy, so the pitch teaches them how to choose. [Step labels S, flag]
4. **Positioning statement (Geoffrey Moore, Crossing the Chasm, 1991; 3rd ed. 2014).** For / Who / The [product] is a [category] / That / Unlike / Our product. Plus the beachhead: pick one segment where you can win whole-product leadership, then expand through adjacent "bowling pins". For a platform PM, the beachhead is a segment plus a use case, not a market.
5. **Category design (Lochhead, Peterson, Ramadan, Maney, Play Bigger, 2016).** Argues that companies win by defining and naming a new problem and category. Counterpoint: Dunford says category creation is slow, expensive, and creators often lose to later entrants; Moore's pragmatists prefer known categories. Teach as a debate with a decision test.
6. **Messaging framework and message house (practitioner tools).** Messaging is the external, persona-specific expression of positioning. Framework fields: core value proposition, pains, benefits per pain, capabilities, proof points. Message house: roof (umbrella message), three pillars (value themes), proof points under each, foundation (credibility and trust facts). One house per offer; one variant per persona.
7. **Pragmatic Institute framework.** 37 boxes, 7 categories. Positioning is an internal document describing the product by its ability to solve market problems, feeding external messages per buyer or persona. PM owns market understanding and direction; PMM frequently leads positioning, launch coordination and enablement.
8. **The offer.** What is sold, separate from what is built: SKUs, editions, bundles, add-ons, units of charge, terms (contract length, commitments, overage, true-up), eligibility (who may buy which edition, regions, partner vs. direct), entitlements and limits. Packaging logic comes from Chapter 15; this chapter turns it into a sellable definition.
9. **Offer readiness and quote-to-cash.** Stages: offer configuration, pricing, quoting, contract management, orders, payment and collections, billing, revenue recognition, renewal (Zuora's list). Revenue recognition follows the five-step IFRS 15 / ASC 606 model, so bundles and variable pricing need finance review before launch. Legal readiness: terms, data processing, AI-specific terms (data use for training, acceptable use, human-in-the-loop commitments).
10. **Claims substantiation (FTC 1984 policy statement).** Reasonable basis before dissemination; express claims of a level of proof need that level; required rigor scales with consequences of a false claim. Workado shows the evidence must match the population the claim covers and be retained.
11. **AI-washing.** Overstating AI use or AI capability. Regulators: FTC (Operation AI Comply 2024; Workado 2025; DoNotPay final order 2025), SEC (Delphia and Global Predictions 2024; Presto 2025), self-regulation (NAD Apple Intelligence 2025). 2025 shift: FTC vacated Rytr order citing the AI Action Plan, but kept deceptive-claim enforcement.

## (e) Beacon worked example ideas (consistent with beacon-case.md and Chapter 01)
- **Starting point.** Chapter 01 discovery found the most-cited cost is on-call burnout and attrition, ahead of MTTR. Priya (champion) cares about keeping her team; Marcus (economic buyer) cares about MTTR because his CFO does. Positioning must serve the champion's value and give the buyer a CFO story.
- **Scope decision (Dunford prep).** Position Beacon Agents as a product, not the Northlight suite, because the strategic tension favours staying tool-agnostic; note the Observe-data advantage as a differentiated capability for Observe customers only.
- **Competitive alternatives.** Main alternative: humans plus runbooks plus chat (status quo). Others: AIOps features bundled in large observability platforms (the switchers left for bundling and procurement simplicity), incident-management tools adding AI, ops-agent startups.
- **Differentiated capabilities.** Root cause with cited evidence across logs, metrics, traces, deploys and change tickets; supervised remediation with an approval gate and allowlisted autonomy; per-action audit logs, scoped credentials and kill switch; learning loop from post-incident reviews; tool-agnostic connectors (including MCP).
- **Value themes (pillars).** (1) Fewer 3 a.m. wake-ups and shorter ones: protect the on-call team (burnout). (2) Faster diagnosis, lower MTTR: the CFO story. (3) Safe to let it act: trust and control that security can approve.
- **Best-fit customers.** SRE and platform teams of 20 to 100 engineers at enterprises with a heavy incident load and an existing correlation footprint (from the Chapter 01 hypothesis); champion is the SRE lead.
- **Market category.** Debate: create "autonomous operations" (category design) vs. "AI incident response for SRE teams" as a subcategory of incident management (big fish, small pond). Recommend the subcategory: buyers have budget and shortlists there; the status quo is the real competitor.
- **Positioning statement (draft, Moore template).** For SRE and platform teams who lose sleep and hours to incident diagnosis, Beacon is an AI incident response agent that finds the likely root cause with cited evidence and runs approved fixes. Unlike runbooks and chat war rooms, Beacon gives the on-call engineer a diagnosis in minutes and only acts with human approval.
- **Message house.** Roof: "Give your on-call team their nights back." Pillars as above. Foundation: audit logs, scoped credentials, kill switch, AI Act documentation (objection-handling layer for Sam).

Define "resolved incident" contractually (who decides, dispute window, exclusions); MSP eligibility and per-customer billing for Leo; preview vs. GA labels for Northlight Live.
