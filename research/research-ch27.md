## (a) Key concepts and frameworks

### 1. What the PM owes the field (synthesis; the chapter's spine)
No single originator. The Pragmatic Framework (Pragmatic Institute) places Sales Alignment, Sales Tools, Content and Channel Training under "Programs" and Win/Loss Analysis under market understanding, which gives the PM a defensible split: the PM owns the truth (problem, persona, differentiation, roadmap, limits); product marketing owns the packaging and campaign; enablement owns the delivery and certification. Seismic's definition (people, processes, technology to improve sales productivity) reminds the PM that content is one third of enablement.

Sales enablement kit, proposed contents (author synthesis, to be stated as the book's template, not attributed):
1. One-page positioning: who it is for, the problem, the alternative they use today, why we win, proof.
2. Pitch narrative (problem first, then change in the world, then capability, then proof).
3. Discovery guide: question bank per persona, built on Rackham's SPIN types (below).
4. Objection handling guide (format below).
5. Competitive battlecards (format below).
6. Demo script and environment (Great Demo! principles).
7. Pricing and packaging guidance: list, discount bands, who approves, what not to promise.
8. ROI or business value tool: inputs the buyer provides, assumptions with sources, conservative default.
9. Roadmap guidance: what can be said, to whom, under NDA, with the "not a commitment" rule.
10. Known limits and "do not sell into" list.
11. FAQ and escalation path to the PM.
Enablement cadence (author synthesis): launch tier sets depth (Tier 1 live training plus certification; Tier 2 recorded session plus kit update; Tier 3 release note). Recurring: monthly field update, quarterly roadmap briefing, battlecards refreshed when a competitor ships or win-loss shows a new pattern. Crayon's 2026 survey associates weekly or faster CI sharing with higher reported revenue impact (79% versus 41%): correlational, but supports a weekly compete feed.

### 2. SPIN discovery (Neil Rackham, Huthwaite, 1988)
Based on about 35,000 observed sales calls over 12 years. Four question types: Situation (facts about current state), Problem (difficulties and dissatisfactions), Implication (consequences of the problem, which build urgency), Need-payoff (value of solving it, stated by the buyer). [Definitions M.] PM application: write the Implication and Need-payoff questions, because they encode the product's value hypothesis; for Beacon, "What happens to revenue when an incident runs 90 minutes instead of 20?"

### 3. The Challenger Sale (Matthew Dixon and Brent Adamson, CEB, 2011)
Reps fall into five profiles; the Challenger is the top performer. Commonly summarized as teach, tailor, take control [M]. PM application: the "teach" is a commercial insight the PM can supply: a reframe of the buyer's problem grounded in product data (for Beacon, that most incident time is spent diagnosing, not fixing; this would need Beacon's own data, see flags).

### 4. Battlecards (Klue framing) and win-loss (Clozd, Pragmatic)
Klue (Mercer 2016): battlecards serve late-stage, head-to-head decisions; frame points from the buyer's need; be specific; arm reps against objections the competitor plants; persona versus competitor, not feature grids. Battlecard format (book template, synthesis): competitor in one line; where they win; where we win; landmines to set (discovery questions that expose their weakness); traps they set and responses; proof points; pricing posture; last updated and owner.
Win-loss (Clozd): neutral third party, wins and losses, within a month of close, about 25 to 30 interviews per segment, tag decision drivers, share continuously. In 2026 an AI harness can run the coding and pattern-finding across transcripts; humans still run high-value interviews.

### 5. Objection handling guide (format, synthesis)
Per objection: the objection verbatim; what it usually means (the underlying concern); clarifying question to ask; response with proof; when to escalate to the PM; when the objection is valid and we should walk away. Classes for B2B platforms: price or value; trust and risk (security, AI safety); integration; "we can build it"; timing; incumbent good enough.

### 6. Demo (Great Demo! method)
Discovery before demo; tailor to what the customer needs; reverse chronology, show the end result first, then how to get there. PM owes a stable demo environment with realistic data and a scripted happy path plus "show the failure mode" moment for AI products.

### 7. Partner and channel enablement
MSPs take ongoing responsibility for a client's IT under an SLA with recurring fees (Wikipedia). Large vendors segment partner programs by partner business model, for example reseller, services, managed services, technology (AWS example). PM implication: a reseller needs a sell kit and pricing guidance; an MSP needs operate-at-scale features (multi-tenant views, per-customer billing, margin math) and a technical certification; both need deal registration and an escalation path. Partner enablement plan template (synthesis): partner segments and their job; what each must know, sell, deploy, support; content and training per segment; certification levels; demo and trial environments; commercial terms summary; feedback channel from partners to product; metrics (certified partners, partner-sourced pipeline, time to first partner deal, partner customer health).

### 8. Customer success: definition and Desired Outcome (Lincoln Murphy)
Customer success occurs when the customer achieves their Desired Outcome through their relationship with the vendor (Murphy, 2023 version). Desired Outcome = Goal (Objective + Conditions + Time Frame) + Appropriate Experience; earlier version Required Outcome + Appropriate Experience (2015). Required Outcome is not the product's functional use. PM application: design onboarding and in-product guidance to reach the customer's Goal, and treat the Appropriate Experience per segment (an MSP's AX differs from a 5,000-person enterprise's).

### 9. Lifecycle (Gainsight)
Onboarding and time to value; adoption and value realization; growth and expansion; renewal and advocacy.

### 10. Onboarding and time to value
Gainsight five phases (welcome and setup; education; kickoff and joint plan; early success in first 30 to 60 days; transition) with reviews at 30, 60, 90 days. John and Bush (ProductLed): map steps to the "First Strike"; rate each green, yellow, red; build a straight-line path. Customer onboarding plan template (synthesis): customer goal in Murphy's form; success criteria and first-value milestone with date; roles on both sides; sales-to-CS handoff notes; technical prerequisites (connectors, permissions, security review); week-by-week plan; risks; 30/60/90 checkpoints; exit criteria to steady state.

### 11. Health scores (Gainsight)
Six categories: usage versus entitlement, feature adoption, sentiment, engagement, relationships (exec sponsor), customer value. Revisit when churn surprises you. PM application: the product owns the telemetry for the first two and should own an outcome metric for the sixth (for Beacon: incidents resolved with Beacon's help). Book point: validate the score by checking it against later renewals; a score that does not predict churn is decoration.

### 12. QBRs (Lincoln Murphy for Gainsight, 2014)
Executives both sides; review against last goals; value delivered; scorecard; roadmap and obstacles; feedback; next 90-day goals. Only for top segment; not tactical. PM role: supply a roadmap segment and listen; bring two or three questions to test, not a pitch.

### 13. NRR and GRR as product outcomes
Formulas per Gainsight (2026). NRR includes expansion; GRR excludes it and caps at 100%. Book framing: GRR is mostly a product-value and fit signal (did they keep it), NRR adds a packaging and expansion-path signal (did the product create reasons to buy more). PMs should decompose: churn and contraction reasons by cohort, expansion by trigger.

### 14. The feedback loop: Bain's inner and outer loop (Net Promoter System, Reichheld and Bain)
Inner loop: front line gets feedback immediately and follows up with the customer. Outer loop: a central function gathers, dedupes, triages and routes issues the front line cannot fix; owning team finds root cause and ranks against other work. Map to product: CSMs and sellers close the inner loop; product ops plus PM run the outer loop.

### 15. Feature requests are solutions (Teresa Torres)
Requests are solutions, not opportunities; ask why; check for more than one way to address the opportunity; use requests as prompts for interviews, not direct inputs to the opportunity solution tree.
Feature request intake and triage policy template (synthesis): intake channels (CRM field, CS tool, support, partner portal, CAB, AI call summaries); required fields (customer, segment, ARR band, the problem in the customer's words, current workaround, impact, evidence link); dedupe rule (merge into an existing problem record, attach the new customer as evidence); link to problem or opportunity, never only to a solution; triage cadence (weekly); statuses (new, needs discovery, linked to opportunity, planned, not planned, shipped); service level for acknowledging (for example 5 business days) and for decisions; close the loop at decision and at ship, to the requester and the account team; anti-patterns (ARR-weighted vote counting as the sole prioritizer, promises in deals). The ARR-weighted vote caveat is author judgment.

### 16. Customer advisory boards (Farland Group)
Five characteristics; charter elements; 1 to 2 in-person meetings a year of 1 to 1.5 days plus virtual sessions; senior executives from top strategic accounts; pitch-free and confidential; sales nominates but does not attend; read-outs with owners and dates. CAB charter template follows Farland's charter elements.

## (d) 2025 to 2026 AI-era developments (verified)

- Salesforce State of Sales 2026 (Feb 2026, 4,050 respondents): 54% of sellers have used agents; nearly 9 in 10 plan to by 2027; 51% of leaders with AI say disconnected systems slow them. - Gong (2025 to 2026): call and deal analysis at scale across 1.8 million deals; vendor products now brief reps before calls. Implication for PMs: ask the harness to pull every competitor mention or objection across recent calls via connectors and cluster them; this replaces anecdote in the feature request pile.

Klarna (Feb 2024) claimed its assistant did the work of 700 agents. Teaching point: define "resolved" before you celebrate a deflection rate, which parallels Beacon's per-resolved-incident pricing.

Data work in the chapter should be phrased as questions and prompts, for example: "Across closed-lost Beacon deals in Q3, list the top five decision drivers with call excerpts as evidence."

## (e) Beacon worked example ideas (consistent with beacon-case.md)

- Enablement kit for the Beacon Agents launch at Northlight Live: discovery questions per persona (Marcus on MTTR and headcount leverage, Priya on trust, Sam on least-privilege and audit logs, Leo on margin). SPIN Implication question for Marcus about the cost of long incidents; Need-payoff for Priya about pages avoided.
- Objection handling guide entries: "Your agent will make things worse in production" (answer: human approval gate, allowlisted autonomous actions only, audit logs; acknowledge the hallucinated-root-cause incident honestly and what changed in evals); "Our observability vendor already has AIOps" (tool-agnostic connectors, cited evidence); "Per-resolved-incident pricing is unpredictable" (pricing guidance with caps, bands); "AI Act documentation?" (EU kit from Sam's perspective).
- Battlecard versus "humans plus runbooks plus chat" as the main alternative, plus a generic large-observability-platform AIOps competitor (no real company names).
- ROI tool for Marcus: inputs are customer's incident count, current MTTR, on-call cost; Beacon's assumption ranges labeled as assumptions. Never quote invented benchmark gains.
- Partner enablement plan for MSPs (Leo): multi-tenant views, per-customer billing, margin math under per-resolved-incident pricing, a certification for deploying connectors across 60 customer tenants, partner feedback channel into triage.
- Customer onboarding plan: Goal in Murphy form ("reduce P1 MTTR by an agreed amount within 90 days while keeping change approval with the on-call lead"); First Strike = first incident where Diagnose's root cause was accepted; 30/60/90 checkpoints on diagnosis acceptance rate and weekly active responders; security review with Sam as prerequisite.
- Health score: usage versus entitled environments; weekly active responders; diagnosis acceptance rate trend; autonomous resolution allowlist breadth; exec sponsor; North Star per account (incidents resolved with Beacon's help per week). Validate against renewals.
- NRR 118% decomposition: separate GRR from expansion; the Beacon Classic sunset is a GRR risk; the pricing move is an NRR mechanism. Do not invent a GRR figure; if needed, the chapter should say "the team computes GRR" or propose that beacon-case.md add one.
- Feature request triage: 40 requests for "auto-rollback without approval" deduped into one problem record: "slow approvals at 3 a.m. extend incidents" (Dana). Solutions considered: on-call mobile approval, broader allowlist, time-boxed auto-approve. Close the loop with each account.
- CAB charter for Beacon: 10 to 12 senior ops leaders (size is a book suggestion, Farland gives no number), twice yearly plus virtual sessions, pitch-free, topics include autonomy boundaries and pricing; one MSP seat for Leo-type partners.
- QBR: Beacon's slice shows incidents resolved with Beacon, MTTR trend, acceptance rate, roadmap for next quarter.
- AI in the loop: the PM asks the harness, through CRM, call-recording and support connectors, to cluster objections from the last 90 days of Beacon calls and map them to the battlecard; and to draft a weekly field update.
