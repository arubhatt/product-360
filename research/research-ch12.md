1. MIT OpenCourseWare 15.818 Pricing (Spring 2010), lecture note "Pricing: Economic Value to the Customer". https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/15-818-pricing-spring-2010/2318b493c551d7770340c79ff6de0c7a_MIT15_818S10_lec02.pdf (fetched 2026-10-30) [V]
   - Definition: customer buys A only if Value_A minus Price_A is at least Value_B minus Price_B. Rearranged: Price_A is at most Differentiation value (A over B) plus Price_B. EVC = reference price of closest alternative + differentiation value. EVC is a ceiling, not a price.
   - Worked example (fictional "Atlantic Computer"): differentiation value $6,000 (labor $4,000, electricity $500, licenses $1,500); competitor price $6,800; EVC $12,800; firm priced at $9,800 by giving away 50% of differentiation value.
   - Implementation steps: (1) define the benefit, not the feature (Xerox priced per copy); (2) identify the honest closest alternative and its real (discounted) price, get units and time horizon right; (3) list specific sources of differentiation value; (4) measure them (independent lab, customer benchmarking studies); (5) add reference + differentiation.
   - Price closer to EVC when: end-benefit effect, shared-cost effect, advantageous mental account. Price well below EVC when: uncertainty about whether attributes are realized (and other effects listed later in the note).
   - Practical warning in the note: a backup company computed an EVC 100% above its price but ignored that rivals discounted 80% from list.

## (a) Key concepts and frameworks

### 1. Value proposition canvas (Osterwalder, Pigneur, Bernarda and Smith; Strategyzer; book Value Proposition Design, 2014)
- Two halves. Customer profile (circle) for ONE segment: Customer jobs (functional, social, emotional; plus supporting jobs), Pains (bad outcomes, obstacles, risks), Gains (required, expected, desired, unexpected outcomes). Value map (square): Products and services; Pain relievers; Gain creators.
- Rank jobs, pains and gains by importance (third-party guides report Strategyzer suggests a scale from insignificant to extreme [S]).
- Fit: when pain relievers and gain creators address the pains and gains that matter most. Strategyzer stresses fit is a claim until customers confirm it [V]. Fit progression in the book [general knowledge]: problem-solution fit (on paper), product-market fit (in the market), business model fit (profitably).
- B2B/platform application: draw one canvas per buying-group role, not one per account. In enterprise the user (Dana), champion (Priya), economic buyer (Marcus) and gatekeeper (Sam) have different jobs. The economic buyer's gains feed the EVC model; the gatekeeper's pains (audit, least privilege) are often the binding constraint. For a platform, draw a separate canvas for the partner (Leo) whose job is margin.
- Template for the book (exact canvas fields): Segment / role; Jobs (ranked); Pains (ranked, each with a measurable form); Gains (ranked, each with a measurable form); Products and services; Pain relievers (each tagged to a pain); Gain creators (each tagged to a gain); Evidence for fit (interview, usage, pilot data); Untested assumptions.

### 2. Economic value to the customer (EVC), also "economic value estimation" (Nagle and Holden; Nagle and Müller, The Strategy and Tactics of Pricing, 6th edition 2018, Routledge [edition details general knowledge]; teaching formulation in MIT 15.818 note)
- EVC = reference value (price of the customer's next best alternative, NBA) + differentiation value (monetary value of every way you differ from the NBA, positive and negative). EVC is a ceiling, not a price [V MIT].
- Steps [V MIT, adapted]: (1) define the benefit, not the feature; (2) identify the honest NBA from the customer's view, with its real discounted price, units and time horizon; (3) list differentiation drivers, positive and negative; (4) quantify each with evidence (benchmarks, pilots, customer studies); (5) sum; (6) choose how much value to leave with the customer.
- Nagle's distinction between monetary value (cost savings, revenue gains) and psychological value [general knowledge from Nagle; Deloitte-hosted PDF of 6th edition now 404]. B2B work is mostly monetary value.
- When to price close to EVC versus well below it [V MIT]: closer when the product is a small part of an essential end use, when cost is shared or paid by someone else, when it sits in an advantageous budget; well below when the customer is uncertain the benefit will be realized. For AI products, uncertainty is the dominant effect, which argues for pilots, guarantees or outcome-linked pricing.
- How a B2B PM applies it: build the value model with the customer (Marcus) using the customer's own numbers; present negative differentiation openly (adoption effort, risk); show the price as a share of EVC.

### 3. Value-based versus cost-plus versus competitive pricing
- Cost-plus: unit cost times (1 + markup). Simple, defensible, ignores willingness to pay. Wrong for software and AI where marginal cost is low or falling fast (LLMflation, a16z [V]).
- Competitive (market-based): price relative to rivals. Spann, Fischer and Tellis found about 60% of new product launches in their sample followed market pricing patterns [V].
- Value-based: price from EVC by segment. Survey evidence pooled by Hinterhuber: only about 17% of firms value-based [S].
- Practical synthesis: value sets the ceiling, competition sets the reference, cost sets the floor. Price sits in the corridor between floor and EVC.

### 4. DCF and NPV for product investments (standard corporate finance; Brealey, Myers and Allen; Gallo HBR refresher [V intro])
- Concept only: a future dollar is worth less than a present one. NPV = sum of each period's expected net cash flow discounted at the firm's required return (hurdle rate, often based on cost of capital), minus the upfront investment. NPV above zero means the project is expected to earn more than the hurdle.
- What a PM must understand, not compute: (1) the drivers (adoption curve, price, margin, cost to build and run, time to revenue); (2) the discount rate is set by finance, and riskier bets carry higher rates; (3) late cash flows are heavily discounted, so faster time to value matters; (4) sensitivity: which two assumptions flip the sign; (5) scenarios (base, downside, upside) instead of one number; (6) the counterfactual baseline (Christensen, Kaufman and Shih "DCF trap": doing nothing is rarely flat [S]); (7) real options: staged investment with go/no-go gates is worth more than a single NPV suggests (Forrester's "flexibility" component expresses the same idea [V]).
- AI harness practice: the PM asks the harness to build the NPV model from stated assumptions and to run sensitivity and scenarios; the PM's job is to defend the assumptions with evidence and to read the result.
- Investment case (NPV summary) template: Decision asked; Options (including do nothing, with declining baseline); Key assumptions with evidence and confidence; Cash flow summary by year (revenue, gross margin including inference, investment); Discount rate (from finance); NPV, payback period, IRR if finance uses it; Sensitivity (top 3 drivers); Scenarios; Stage gates and kill criteria; Non-financial value and risks.

### 5. Pricing across the life cycle (Dean, HBR 1950 [V citation]; Spann, Fischer and Tellis 2015 [V])
- Introduction: skimming (high price, harvest inelastic early adopters, step down) versus penetration (low price to build share fast, exploit scale and network effects). Conditions [S summary of Dean and standard texts]: skim when demand is inelastic, value is uncertain to imitate, capacity is limited, segments separable; penetrate when demand is elastic, scale or network effects strong, imitation fast.
- Growth: segment and tier; introduce good/better/best; protect price integrity as sales scale.
- Maturity: competition compresses differentiation value; shift to bundling, packaging, cost discipline, price for retention (NRR), and add new differentiation.
- Decline: harvest (hold or raise price on loyal base), migrate (price incentives to move to successor), or sunset with notice. B2B nuance: contractual commitments and migration cost are the customer's switching cost; a sunset price plan is also a trust plan.
- Evidence: most firms neither skim nor penetrate deliberately (about 20% did, about 60% priced to market) [V].
- Lifecycle pricing plan template: Stage and evidence for it; Pricing objective (share, margin, retention, migration); Price level relative to EVC; Packaging and value metric; Discount guardrails; Trigger to move to the next stage; Customer communication; Risks.

### 6. Behavioral pricing (evidence and ethics)
- Decoy / asymmetric dominance: Huber, Payne and Puto 1982 [V citation]. Limits: Frederick, Lee and Baskin 2014 find it largely confined to numeric, stylized displays [V]. Pricing tables are numeric, so the effect is plausible there.
- Anchoring: Tversky and Kahneman 1974 (Science) [general knowledge, not fetched]; Ariely, Loewenstein and Prelec 2003: arbitrary SSN anchors shifted WTP 57% to 107% while relative rankings stayed coherent [V].
- Charm pricing: Anderson and Simester 2003, $9 endings raised demand in three field experiments, stronger for new items and low-information shoppers [V]. Minimal relevance for negotiated enterprise deals.
- Bundling: Adams and Yellen 1976 (pure versus mixed bundling) [V citation].
- Ethics line: FTC 2022 four categories of dark patterns [V]; EU Article 6a genuine price reductions [V]. Rule for the book: a behavioral tactic is acceptable when every option is real and purchasable, the full price is disclosed upfront, and the customer would not feel tricked on learning how the page was designed.

### 7. ROI and business value models for B2B buyers
- TCO: all costs of acquiring, deploying, operating and retiring a solution over its life (Gartner term; Gartner page blocked, definition paraphrased from TechTarget and others [S]). For AI products add: inference and usage overage, evaluation and oversight time, data preparation, change management, integration upkeep.
- Forrester TEI pattern [V]: benefits, costs, flexibility, risk; risk-adjusted; composite organization built from customer interviews; disclosure of commissioning.
- Customer ROI calculator spec (template): Audience (economic buyer); Value drivers (each with formula in words, input owner, default from benchmark and source); Cost side (subscription, usage, services, internal effort); Time to value and ramp; Risk adjustment (haircut per driver); Outputs (net benefit, ROI, payback months, NPV at the customer's rate); Assumption log editable by the customer; Evidence and citation for every default; Disclosure (who built it, what is modeled versus measured); Post-sale tracking plan (same drivers measured in product).

### 8. AI products' value offering (2025 to 2026)
- Five charge models (Bessemer 2026 [V]): seat, usage, workflow, outcome, hybrid. Stripe separates the value metric from the charge metric [V].
- Outcome pricing needs an unambiguous, measurable outcome and tolerance for cost variance [V Bessemer]. Definitions matter: Intercom now bills per "outcome" with a three-part definition and a monthly minimum [V].
- AI gross margins about 50% to 60% versus 80% to 90% SaaS (Bessemer characterization [V as claim]); inference cost per capability falling about 10x per year [V a16z]. Price on value, guard on cost.
- AI spend comes partly from labor budgets, so the reference value for an agent can be human labor cost, not software cost. This raises EVC but also raises scrutiny.
